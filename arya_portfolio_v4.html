The insight
Users weren't confused. They were anxious.

Insurance users come to the platform when something has gone wrong. They're stressed, sometimes dealing with real loss. Errors weren't a UI problem — they were an anxiety problem.

Black box claims experience

Users submitted claims and waited with zero visibility. Anxiety peaked during the wait — errors concentrated here.

System-centric IA

Platform organized around how the insurance company processes claims — not how a stressed human thinks about their situation.

Fragmented platform

470+ screens built feature by feature. No shared design language. Changes slow, inconsistent, and expensive.

Long processing cycles

Manual workflows, unclear ownership, poor task clarity creating long cycle times for users and internal teams.

Design approach
Claim Clarity — radical transparency at the worst moment.

Every user should know exactly where their claim is, what's happening right now, what comes next — not in a status page, as the primary surface.

1

Emotional journey mapping

Research built around anxiety, not just usability

Recruited users who had filed claims within the last 30 days. Conducted emotional journey mapping — not just what steps did you take but what were you feeling at each step. What scared you. Where did you feel lost. The research revealed anxiety concentration points that traditional usability testing would have missed entirely.
2

Claim Clarity concept

Milestone visualization · Proactive communication · Decision support

Three pillars integrated into one experience — milestone visualization showing claim journey as the primary UI surface, proactive communication reaching out before users needed to reach out, and decision support surfacing exactly what information was needed at the right moment. Not three separate features — one coherent experience.
3

Design system from scratch

470+ interfaces · 35 user flows · full component library

Built a complete design system covering all 470+ interfaces across 35 user flows. Led and mentored the design team throughout delivery — embedded continuous UX research at every sprint. The design system was the single source of truth that made the scale manageable.

The challenge
Government financial reporting — decisions at scale

Alberta government financial teams managing complex data across siloed reporting tools — no unified view, no decision pathways, dashboards built for data presentation rather than decision support.

Data without insight

Enormous volumes of financial data with no hierarchy, no decision signals, no indication of what required action. Information density without decision clarity.

Siloed reporting tools

Financial data spread across disconnected tools — no coherent single view. Every report a separate exercise in manual reconciliation.

Multi-level user complexity

Analysts, directors, deputy ministers, and ministers — all on the same platform with completely different information needs and decision contexts.

No validated design process

Design decisions on assumptions — no user research program, no usability testing, no evidence-based synthesis connecting behavior to design decisions.

Research program
Understanding how government makes financial decisions

75+ in-depth interviews. 220+ participants across 6 usability rounds. Contextual inquiry during actual monthly reporting cycles — real workflows under real pressure.

Design strategy
From data reporting to decision intelligence

North star: every government financial user should understand their position and know what action to take — in the time they actually have available.

1

Role-adaptive dashboard architecture

Same data. Three views. Analyst · Director · Executive.

Built a role-adaptive system — three distinct view configurations presenting the same underlying financial data through completely different lenses. Analyst view: full data depth, trend analysis, variance drill-down. Director view: exception-based — show me what is off-target and what needs my attention. Executive view: three to five key indicators with clear signal strength. Progressive disclosure built into every layer.

User level Primary need Design principle
Financial analystsFull data depth and drill-downDensity with clear hierarchy
DirectorsException-based alertingSignal before data
Deputy ministersStrategic position clarityThree signals maximum
2

45+ dashboard templates

Expenditure · Revenue · Variance · Budget · Compliance · Capital projects

Designed 45+ dashboard templates covering the full spectrum of government financial reporting: expenditure tracking against budget by department, program and period; revenue monitoring with variance to forecast; year-end budget consumption with projection to close; compliance reporting for Treasury Board submissions; grant and transfer payment tracking; capital project expenditure monitoring. Each template built with Signal — Context — Action information architecture. All on a shared visualization component library with a calibrated government financial data color system.

3

Executive design strategy presentation

Presented to senior executive and deputy minister-level leadership — adopted in full

Design strategy presented to senior executive leadership as a business case — every recommendation framed in terms of decision quality, time-to-decision, error reduction, and compliance improvement. Included quantified estimates of time saved per user per reporting cycle, projected improvement in budget variance detection speed, and a roadmap connecting design investment to measurable government financial outcomes. Adopted in full by leadership.
