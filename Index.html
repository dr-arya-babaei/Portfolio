Case Study · Apr 2020 — Dec 2022

Rebuilding HSBC Canada's Investment Banking Experience

A zero-to-one design transformation — from fragmented platform to unified investment intelligence for 500,000+ monthly users during the pandemic.

Context
A platform under pressure

April 2020. Pandemic hits. Branches close overnight. The digital platform becomes the only channel for half a million Canadians managing mortgages, credit, and investments. No design team. No research program. No design system. Everything built from scratch.

HSBC Canada was acquired by RBC in 2024. The 780,000 retail customers rebuilt through this work are now RBC customers — the platform I designed for them is directly relevant to what RBC Direct Investing is building today.

The problem
Fragmented experience. Broken trust.

Every feature technically worked. But the end-to-end investment journey was broken at every handoff.

Fragmented CX journey

Portfolio, market data, trading, and performance metrics across four disconnected surfaces with no shared context.

Broken trading workflow

11 steps to execute a trade. No market context. No inline education. Anxiety-inducing confirmation. 4 in 10 trades abandoned.

Zero portfolio intelligence

P&L, adjusted cost base, time-weighted return — all buried. Users built personal spreadsheets to reconcile their own money.

No investor education

TFSA room unused. RRSP optimization ignored. Tax loss harvesting unknown. The capability existed — design wasn't surfacing it.

"One user had a spreadsheet she updated manually every week because she didn't trust the platform's view of her own money. These were engaged investors who had completely given up on the product."

Design pillars
Three layers. One north star.

Make users feel like smarter investors every time they open the platform.

1

Portfolio intelligence

Unified view — real-time P&L, cost base, attribution, allocation

Rebuilt the investment home around one idea — answer the three questions every investor asks at login. How am I doing. What's changed. Do I need to act. Real-time valuation, daily P&L, unrealized gains, adjusted cost base, performance attribution by sector, asset allocation ring chart. All in one surface. Progressive disclosure — summary visible immediately, detail on expansion.

Confident execution

Trading workflow — 11 steps to 5, gamified financial literacy

Rebuilt around one concept — every touchpoint actively reduces anxiety. Five steps instead of eleven. Persistent real-time bid-ask spread throughout the entire flow. Inline contextual education at every decision point — market orders, limit orders, stop-loss, trailing stops — surfaced in the moment of doing. Portfolio impact preview before confirmation. Execution card showing price, slippage, and immediate impact. First-ever watchlist-to-trade flow.

Behavioral nudges, financial health score, tax optimization

Embedded education and behavioral nudges surfaced contextually within the normal investment journey. TFSA contribution gaps, tax loss harvesting prompts, rebalancing signals — all triggered by behavior, not buried in a learn section. Financial Health Score showing diversification quality, registered account optimization, cash drag ratio, and risk alignment.

Case Study · Jan 2024 — Present · KPMG Canada

AI-Integrated Enterprise Financial Platform — Enbridge

Zero-to-one design for a 9-module enterprise financial platform — design vision, AI-integrated research, 100+ component design system, 89% adoption in 3 months.

Context
Nine modules. One platform. Zero coherence.

Enbridge needed a complete enterprise financial platform built from zero — 9 modules, hundreds of workflows, users from frontline operators to CFO-level executives. Every business unit had a different definition of what the platform should do. No shared design language. No coherent user experience. No design system.

The core insight from stakeholder research — every business unit described the same problem in different language. Users were drowning in data but starving for insight. That became the design north star for the entire platform.

The problem
Data everywhere. Decisions nowhere.
9Disconnected modules
0Shared design language
LowInitial task completion
ManualResearch synthesis — 2 weeks

No coherent platform vision

9 modules built independently with no shared interaction language, information architecture, or visual system.

Research bottleneck

80+ user interviews generating insights too slowly. Findings arriving after decisions were already made — too late to influence.

No design system

Every module built ad hoc. No shared components. Engineering translating designs manually — slow, inconsistent, expensive.

Multi-segment user complexity

Frontline operators, mid-level managers, and CFO-level executives — all on the same platform with conflicting mental models and needs.

Design approach
From data to decision — the platform north star.

Every module, surface, and component evaluated against one question — does this help someone make a better decision faster, or does it just give them more information?

1

AI-integrated research pipeline

LLM-assisted synthesis — 2 weeks to 2 days

80+ user interviews across all user segments. The traditional synthesis process was a bottleneck — 2 weeks of manual affinity mapping meant insights arrived after decisions were closed. Implemented LLM-assisted synthesis using Claude — structured analysis prompts processing interview transcripts, identifying patterns, contradictions, and priority themes. Research cycle cut from 2 weeks to 2 days. 4 usability rounds across v1, v2, v3 releases with insights feeding directly into sprint planning.

2

Signal · Context · Action framework

Concept architecture governing all 9 modules

Defined a three-layer concept architecture as the design constitution for the entire platform. Signal — the most important information surfaced immediately with clear visual hierarchy. Context — background needed to understand the signal. Action — next step made obvious and frictionless. Every component, every interaction pattern, every piece of UX writing evaluated against this framework. Frontline operators responded to Signal. Executives needed Context. Everyone needed Action.

3

100+ component design system

Co-owned with engineering from token level

Built a 100+ component library with full Figma variables, dev mode specs, and complete documentation. Engineering brought in at the token level from day one — not after the fact. Components built to handle financial data density across all 9 module types. Result: engineers implementing directly, not translating. Adoption hit 89% within 3 months because the handoff friction was essentially zero.

Case Study · May 2021 — Dec 2023 · SIA Partners USA

Consumer Financial Platform — Brown & Brown Insurance

Complete redesign of a consumer financial services platform — 470+ interfaces, 35 user flows, full design system, continuous UX research embedded throughout delivery.

Context
Users in their worst moments.

Insurance is unique as a design context — users come to the platform when something has gone wrong. They've had an accident, a loss, a crisis. They're stressed, confused, and the last thing they need is a complicated system standing between them and resolution. The existing platform was making a hard moment harder.

The core research insight — users weren't making errors because the interface was confusing. They were making errors because they were anxious. That single reframe changed the entire concept direction.

The problem
Complexity at the worst possible moment.
HighClaim processing errors
LowUser satisfaction
LongWorkflow cycle time
0Shared design system

Black box claims experience

Users submitted claims and waited with zero visibility into status, progress, or next steps. Anxiety peaked during the wait — errors concentrated here.

System-centric information architecture

Platform organized around how the insurance company processes claims — not how a stressed human being thinks about their situation.

Fragmented platform — 470+ screens

Built feature by feature with no coherent design language. Every screen a one-off. Changes slow, inconsistent, and expensive.

Long processing cycles

Manual workflows, unclear ownership, and poor task clarity creating long cycle times for both users and internal teams.

"Users weren't failing because the interface was hard to use. They were failing because they were scared and the platform gave them no signals that everything was going to be okay."

Design approach
Claim Clarity — radical transparency at the worst moment.

Every user should know exactly where their claim is, what's happening right now, what comes next, and what if anything they need to do — not as a status page, but as the primary surface of the entire experience.

1

Emotional journey mapping

Research built around anxiety, not just usability

Recruited users who had filed claims within the last 30 days. Conducted emotional journey mapping — not just what steps did you take but what were you feeling at each step. What scared you. Where did you feel lost. Where did the system feel like it was working against you. That research revealed the anxiety concentration points that traditional usability testing would have missed entirely.

2

Claim Clarity concept

Radical transparency as the primary design principle

Rebuilt the claims experience around three pillars — milestone visualization showing the claim's journey through stages as the primary UI surface, proactive communication reaching out to users before they needed to reach out to us, and decision support surfacing exactly what information was needed at exactly the right moment. All three integrated into a single coherent experience — not three separate features bolted together.

3

Design system from scratch

470+ interfaces · 35 user flows · full component library

Built a complete design system from zero covering all 470+ interfaces across 35 user flows. Led and mentored the design team throughout delivery — embedded continuous UX research at every sprint, drove alignment with Product, Engineering, and leadership. The design system became the single source of truth that made the scale of this engagement manageable.
